Efficient Wheat Head Segmentation with Minimal Annotation: A Generative Approach

Jaden Myers, Keyhan Najafian, Farhad Maleki, Katie Ovens

Closes the simulation-to-reality gap with a modified CycleGAN. A large computationally simulated annotated dataset is translated into realistic field imagery, and the resulting model is refined through pseudo-labeling without additional manual annotation.

Abstract

Deep learning models have been used for a variety of image processing tasks. However, most of these models are developed through supervised learning approaches, which rely heavily on the availability of large-scale annotated datasets. Developing such datasets is tedious and expensive. In the absence of an annotated dataset, synthetic data can be used for model development; however, due to the substantial differences between simulated and real data, a phenomenon referred to as domain gap, the resulting models often underperform when applied to real data. In this research, we aim to address this challenge by first computationally simulating a large-scale annotated dataset and then using a generative adversarial network (GAN) to fill the gap between simulated and real images.

Key contributions

  • Adapts CycleGAN so that image translation preserves the geometry required for the paired segmentation masks to stay valid.
  • Combines computational simulation, unpaired image translation, and pseudo-label fine-tuning into one annotation-free pipeline.
  • Demonstrates that generative domain translation improves over training on raw simulated data.

Methods and techniques

  • Modified CycleGAN
  • Unpaired image-to-image translation
  • Computational data simulation
  • Pseudo-labeling and fine-tuning
  • Semantic segmentation

Data and materials

  • Computationally simulated annotated wheat field images
  • Real unannotated wheat field imagery

Applications

  • Annotation-free segmentation model development for crops
  • Synthetic-to-real transfer in agricultural imaging

Related work in Precision Agriculture

A Semi-Self-Supervised Learning Approach for Wheat Head Detection Using Extremely Small Number of Labeled Samples

ICCVW 2021

Trains a wheat head detector from three contoured images and a few minutes of handheld video. The annotated wheat heads are cut out, augmented, and pasted onto background field footage to simulate a large computationally annotated dataset, and two domain adaptation stages then close the gap to real imagery.

Semi-Self-Supervised Learning for Semantic Segmentation in Images with Dense Patterns

Plant Phenomics 2023

Extends the synthesis-plus-domain-adaptation idea from detection to pixel-level segmentation. Two annotated images and one short video clip are enough to train a customized U-Net that reaches a Dice score of 0.89 internally and generalizes to wheat fields from 18 domains across five countries.

Detection of Fusarium Damaged Kernels in Wheat Using Deep Semi-Supervised Learning on a Novel WheatSeedBelt Dataset

ICCVW 2023

Introduces WheatSeedBelt, a 40,420-image single-kernel dataset covering 268 wheat varieties with damage annotations, and uses it to train semi-supervised classifiers for Fusarium damaged kernel grading. An inter-rater study shows human experts do not outperform the model.
