Beyond Annotations: Efficient Wheat Head Segmentation Using L-Systems, Game Engines, and Student-Teacher Models

Hosein Beheshtifard, Elijah Mickelson, Keyhan Najafian, Farhad Maleki

Builds the training set from a plant growth model instead of from photographs. Lindenmayer systems describe wheat morphology, a game engine renders the scenes with perfect masks, and a student-teacher loop adapts the resulting model to real fields, reaching a Dice score of 0.87 with no manual annotation.

Abstract

The increasing global population necessitates efficient monitoring of wheat fields to ensure food security. Traditional manual methods for crop monitoring are labor-intensive and inefficient, hindering the adoption of precision agriculture techniques. Deep learning-based semantic segmentation has shown promise in tasks such as yield prediction, crop health monitoring, and disease detection. However, the creation of large-scale annotated datasets required for training these models is resource-intensive. This paper proposes a self-supervised approach for wheat head segmentation, leveraging Lindenmayer systems (L-systems) to generate a synthetic dataset and employing a student-teacher model for domain adaptation. The methodology involves training a semantic segmentation model on synthetic data and refining it with pseudo-labeled real data. Our results demonstrate significant improvements in segmentation performance, achieving a Dice score of 0.87, setting a new benchmark in wheat head segmentation without requiring manual annotation.

Key contributions

  • Uses procedural plant modeling with L-systems to encode wheat morphology rather than sampling it from photographs.
  • Renders synthetic scenes in a game engine, which yields pixel-perfect masks for free.
  • Applies student-teacher domain adaptation with pseudo-labeled real imagery.
  • Achieves a Dice score of 0.87 with no manual annotation.

Related work in Precision Agriculture

A Semi-Self-Supervised Learning Approach for Wheat Head Detection Using Extremely Small Number of Labeled Samples

ICCVW 2021

Trains a wheat head detector from three contoured images and a few minutes of handheld video. The annotated wheat heads are cut out, augmented, and pasted onto background field footage to simulate a large computationally annotated dataset, and two domain adaptation stages then close the gap to real imagery.

Semi-Self-Supervised Learning for Semantic Segmentation in Images with Dense Patterns

Plant Phenomics 2023

Extends the synthesis-plus-domain-adaptation idea from detection to pixel-level segmentation. Two annotated images and one short video clip are enough to train a customized U-Net that reaches a Dice score of 0.89 internally and generalizes to wheat fields from 18 domains across five countries.

Detection of Fusarium Damaged Kernels in Wheat Using Deep Semi-Supervised Learning on a Novel WheatSeedBelt Dataset

ICCVW 2023

Introduces WheatSeedBelt, a 40,420-image single-kernel dataset covering 268 wheat varieties with damage annotations, and uses it to train semi-supervised classifiers for Fusarium damaged kernel grading. An inter-rater study shows human experts do not outperform the model.
