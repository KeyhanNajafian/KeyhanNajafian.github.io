Integument colour change: Tracking delayed growth of Oppia nitens as a sub-lethal indicator of soil toxicity

Olukayode O. Jegede, Hamzat O. Fajana, Adedamola Adedokun, Keyhan Najafian, Lingling Jin, Ian Stavness, Steven D. Siciliano

Turns the colour of a soil mite's shell into a measurable toxicity endpoint. Image analysis of how the integument of Oppia nitens darkens from amber to brown tracks growth, detecting sublethal heavy metal and organic contamination within seven days.

Abstract

Growth is an important toxicity end-point in ecotoxicology but is rarely used in soil ecotoxicological studies. Here, we assessed the growth change of Oppia nitens when exposed to reference and heavy metal toxicants. To assess mite growth, we developed an image analysis methodology to measure colour spectrum changes of the mite integument at the final developmental stage, as a proxy for growth change. We linked the values of red, green, blue, key-black, and light colour of mites to different growth stages. Our method of mite colour change detected sublethal effects within seven days of exposure at levels close to those that inhibit reproduction. At sublethal concentrations, cadmium, copper, zinc, phenanthrene and boric acid delayed the growth of Oppia nitens. As expected, cadmium was the most toxic metal on the growth of the mites. Assessing mite growth changes with image analytic methods is a step in the right direction in exploring additional toxicity endpoints.

Key contributions

  • Establishes integument colour change from amber to dark brown as a measurable proxy for growth in Oppia nitens.
  • Develops an image analysis method linking red, green, blue, key-black, and lightness values to developmental stage.
  • Detects sublethal toxicity within seven days, at concentrations close to those that inhibit reproduction.
  • Adds growth to the small set of usable endpoints in soil ecotoxicology, alongside survival and reproduction.

Methods and techniques

  • Colour spectrum image analysis
  • RGB and key-black colour quantification
  • Soil ecotoxicological bioassay
  • Sublethal dose-response assessment

Data and materials

  • Oppia nitens exposed to cadmium, copper, zinc, phenanthrene, and boric acid
  • Colour measurements of the mite integument at the final developmental stage
  • Growth responses compared against published survival and reproduction endpoints

Results

  • Sublethal growth delay detected within seven days of exposure
  • Growth was as sensitive an endpoint as reproduction
  • Cadmium was the most toxic of the metals tested on mite growth

Applications

  • Soil contamination assessment and environmental remediation
  • Ecotoxicological risk assessment for agricultural soils
  • Image-based phenotyping of soil invertebrates
