A Deep Learning-Based Pipeline for Celiac Disease Diagnosis Using Histopathological Images

Farhad Maleki, Kevin Cote, Keyhan Najafian, Katie Ovens, Yan Miao, Rita Zakarian, Caroline Reinhold, Reza Forghani, Peter Savadjiev, Zu-hua Gao

Diagnoses celiac disease from whole slide images of duodenal biopsies using far fewer slides than a conventional pipeline needs. An Image Assembler synthesizes training examples by recomposing tissue segments onto a blank canvas, and patient-level predictions come from a majority vote across 101 synthesized images.

Abstract

With an increasing number of celiac disease diagnoses and the increasing number of misdiagnoses, automated approaches are valuable to aid pathologists in efficiently diagnosing this disease. Histopathological analysis of intestinal biopsy is considered the gold standard for diagnosis. Convolutional neural networks have achieved promising results for various image processing tasks. A common challenge in medical imaging analysis is obtaining a large number of samples, impeding the full potential of deep learning. In this paper, we propose a classification pipeline to train deep convolutional neural networks to accurately diagnosis celiac disease using models trained with a small number of samples. To show the utility of this approach, we compared it to a typical classification pipeline. The results indicate the superiority of our classification pipeline in distinguishing celiac disease from normal tissue with precision, recall, and accuracy of 0.941, 0.889, and 0.893, respectively. Although we showed the utility of the proposed pipeline for celiac diagnosis, it can also be used for other applications utilizing histopathological imaging.

Key contributions

  • Introduces Image Assembler, which segments tissue from a whole slide image and recomposes random subsets of segments onto a blank canvas to synthesize many labeled training examples per patient.
  • Aggregates the many per-slide predictions into one patient-level diagnosis through a majority vote, avoiding the mislabeling risk of patch-level training.
  • Shows that composing four segments per synthetic image outperforms both single segments and whole slide images on every metric.
  • Provides a pipeline that is independent of the underlying architecture and transferable to other whole slide pathology classification tasks.

Methods and techniques

  • Whole slide image analysis
  • Image Assembler synthesis
  • ResNet-50 pretrained on ImageNet
  • OTSU thresholding and foreground segmentation
  • Oversampling for class imbalance
  • Majority-vote patient-level aggregation
  • Albumentations augmentation

Data and materials

  • 426 digitized duodenal biopsy slides from the McGill University Health Centre Department of Pathology, 2016 to 2019
  • Slides graded by Marsh histotype; ambiguous histotype 2 cases excluded
  • 174 normal and 247 diseased samples after grouping
  • Patient-wise split of 60% training, 20% validation, 20% testing

Results

  • Precision 0.941, recall 0.889, accuracy 0.893 with four segments per synthetic image
  • Precision 0.917, recall 0.815, accuracy 0.831 with single segments
  • Whole slide baseline: precision 0.833, recall 0.833, accuracy 0.786

Applications

  • Computer-aided celiac disease diagnosis from intestinal biopsy
  • Reducing inter-observer variability in histopathological reporting
  • Whole slide pathology classification where large annotated datasets are impractical
