Using Deep Learning to Determine Kidney Function Decline in Patients with ADPKD

Hyun Bae Jang, Caroline Reinhold, Keyhan Najafian, Farhad Maleki, Ahsan Alam

Predicts which patients with autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease will lose kidney function rapidly, by fusing MRI features with clinical variables rather than relying on height-adjusted total kidney volume alone.

Abstract

Autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD) is associated with progressive kidney cyst growth, eventually leading to kidney failure in many patients. The current risk stratification tool uses height-adjusted total kidney volume (TKV) by age. However, it does not include clinical or other imaging features that may contribute to kidney function decline. In this study, we developed a deep learning classifier that integrates clinical and imaging features to predict rapid estimated GFR (eGFR) decline in patients with ADPKD. We included 120 patients with confirmed ADPKD with at least one MRI scan and at least 3 serial eGFR measures. An eGFR decline of 4 mL/min/1.73 m2 per year or greater was defined as rapid progression. We tripled the sample size by extracting the largest manually segmented 2D MRI slice and its two neighbouring slices for each patient. Pixel-level and spatial-level transformations were applied to the images to enhance our deep model's generalizability and robustness. The model consisted of three components: EfficientNet-b2, FuseNet, and a classifier, with a train:validation:test split of 63:17:20. The EfficientNet-b2 extracted 1000 features from each MRI slice. The FuseNet performed feature fusion on 15 clinically relevant features associated with ADPKD. The feature maps obtained from both models were concatenated and fed into the classifier. Weighted-average F1 and AUC scores and the confusion matrix were used to assess the model's performance.

Key contributions

  • Combines imaging and clinical information rather than relying on height-adjusted total kidney volume by age, the current risk stratification standard.
  • Fuses 1000 image features from EfficientNet-b2 with 15 clinically relevant ADPKD variables through a dedicated fusion network.
  • Expands a small cohort by using the largest segmented slice and its two neighbours per patient, with pixel-level and spatial-level augmentation.

Methods and techniques

  • EfficientNet-b2 feature extraction
  • FuseNet clinical feature fusion
  • Multimodal feature concatenation
  • Pixel-level and spatial-level augmentation
  • Weighted-average F1 and AUC evaluation

Data and materials

  • 120 patients with confirmed ADPKD, each with at least one MRI scan and at least three serial eGFR measures
  • Rapid progression defined as eGFR decline of 4 mL/min/1.73 m2 per year or greater
  • Manually segmented 2D MRI slices plus two neighbouring slices per patient
  • Train, validation, and test split of 63:17:20

Applications

  • Risk stratification of ADPKD patients before kidney failure
  • Multimodal prognostic modeling in nephrology
  • Earlier identification of candidates for intervention
