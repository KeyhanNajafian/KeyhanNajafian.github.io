Predicting phenotypes from novel genomic markers using deep learning

Shivani Sehrawat, Keyhan Najafian, Lingling Jin

Genomic selection usually runs on SNP markers. This work shows that structural variants and transposable elements predict crop phenotypes better, using NovGMDeep, a 1D convolutional network evaluated on Arabidopsis thaliana and Oryza sativa.

Abstract

Genomic selection (GS) models use single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) markers to predict phenotypes. However, these predictive models face challenges due to the high dimensionality of genome-wide SNP marker data. Thanks to recent breakthroughs in DNA sequencing and decreased sequencing cost, the study of novel genomic variants such as structural variations (SVs) and transposable elements (TEs) become increasingly prevalent. In this article, we develop a deep convolutional neural network model, NovGMDeep, to predict phenotypes using SVs and TEs markers for GS. The proposed model is trained and tested on samples of Arabidopsis thaliana and Oryza sativa using k-fold cross-validation. The prediction accuracy is evaluated using Pearson's Correlation Coefficient (PCC), mean absolute error (MAE) and SD of MAE. The predicted results showed higher correlation when the model is trained with SVs and TEs than with SNPs. NovGMDeep also has higher prediction accuracy when comparing with conventional statistical models. This work sheds light on the unappreciated function of SVs and TEs in genotype-to-phenotype associations, as well as their extensive significance and value in crop development.

Key contributions

  • Moves genomic selection beyond SNPs by encoding structural variants and transposable elements as one-hot marker representations.
  • Introduces NovGMDeep, a four-layer 1D convolutional network with max pooling and dropout, designed for marker counts far larger than the sample size.
  • Outperforms rrBLUP, gBLUP, DeepGS, and G2Pdeep on Arabidopsis thaliana phenotypes.
  • Documents where the approach fails: with only 176 rice accessions, deep models lose to statistical baselines.

Methods and techniques

  • 1D convolutional neural network
  • Genomic selection
  • One-hot encoding of structural variants and transposable elements
  • 3-fold cross-validation
  • Pearson correlation and mean absolute error evaluation
  • Comparison against rrBLUP, gBLUP, DeepGS, and G2Pdeep

Data and materials

  • Arabidopsis thaliana: 914 accessions with 155,440 structural variant markers, and 923 accessions with SNP markers, from the 1001 Genomes project and the European Variation Archive
  • Phenotypes from AraPheno: flowering time, rosette leaf number, cauline axillary branch number, stem length
  • Oryza sativa subsp. Japonica: 176 accessions with 6,074 transposable element markers and 493,882 SNP markers
  • Rice phenotypes: panicle length, spikelet number, days to heading, plant height

Results

  • Flowering time PCC of 0.788 with structural variants against 0.594 with SNPs
  • Structural variants beat SNPs on all four Arabidopsis phenotypes and beat both statistical baselines
  • Mean absolute error roughly halved for structural variants relative to SNPs
  • Transposable element markers outperformed SNPs in rice, though the small cohort limited deep model performance

Applications

  • Genomic selection and breeding value estimation in crops
  • Genotype-to-phenotype association beyond SNP markers
  • Trait prediction for Arabidopsis and rice breeding programs
