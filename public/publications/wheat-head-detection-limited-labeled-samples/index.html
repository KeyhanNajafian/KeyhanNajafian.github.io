A Semi-Self-Supervised Learning Approach for Wheat Head Detection Using Extremely Small Number of Labeled Samples

Keyhan Najafian, Alireza Ghanbari, Ian Stavness, Lingling Jin, Gholam Hassan Shirdel, Farhad Maleki

Trains a wheat head detector from three contoured images and a few minutes of handheld video. The annotated wheat heads are cut out, augmented, and pasted onto background field footage to simulate a large computationally annotated dataset, and two domain adaptation stages then close the gap to real imagery.

Abstract

Most of the success of deep learning is owed to supervised learning, where a large-scale annotated dataset is used for model training. However, developing such datasets is challenging. In this paper, we develop a semi-self-supervised learning approach for wheat head detection. The proposed method utilized a few short video clips and only one annotated image from each video clip of wheat fields to simulate a large computationally annotated dataset used for model building. Considering the domain gap between the simulated and real images, we applied two domain adaptation steps to alleviate the challenge of distributional shift. The resulting model achieved high performance when applied to real unannotated datasets. When fine-tuned on the dataset from the Global Wheat Head Detection Challenge, the performance was further improved. The model achieved a mean average precision of 0.827, where an overlap of 50% or more between a predicted bounding box and ground truth was considered as a correct prediction. Although the utility of the proposed methodology was shown by applying it to wheat head detection, the proposed method is not limited to this application and could be used for other domains, such as detecting different crop types, alleviating the barrier of lack of large-scale annotated datasets in those domains.

Key contributions

  • Introduces a semi-self-supervised pipeline that generates labeled training data for the target task itself, rather than for an unrelated pretext task.
  • Uses short video clips of wheat fields and of empty background scenes as the raw data source, making data collection nearly free compared with building an annotated image dataset.
  • Applies two sequential domain adaptation stages: strong augmentation of rotated representative images, then pseudo-labeling of real unannotated video frames.
  • Reaches mAP@50 of 0.827 after fine-tuning on the Global Wheat Head Detection dataset, above the 0.741 supervised baseline trained on the same dataset.

Methods and techniques

  • Semi-self-supervised learning
  • Cut-and-paste image synthesis
  • Two-stage domain adaptation
  • Pseudo-labeling
  • YOLO object detection with CIoU loss
  • Stochastic augmentation pipelines (Albumentations)
  • External model evaluation

Data and materials

  • 3 wheat field video clips and 17 background video clips (roughly 11 and 41 minutes)
  • 294,511 extracted background frames; 17,979 wheat field frames; 3 contoured representative images
  • 36,000 computationally annotated synthetic samples
  • Global Wheat Head Detection (GWHD) 2021 dataset, 1,381-image test subset

Results

  • mAP@50 of 0.090 from synthetic data alone, exposing the domain gap
  • mAP@50 of 0.601 after both domain adaptation stages without seeing GWHD
  • mAP@50 of 0.827 after GWHD fine-tuning, versus 0.741 for the supervised baseline

Applications

  • Wheat yield component estimation and spike counting
  • High-throughput plant phenotyping for breeding programs
  • Bootstrapping annotation for other crops and dense-object domains
