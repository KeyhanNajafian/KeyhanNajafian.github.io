Machine learning prediction of brain metastasis invasion pattern on brain magnetic resonance imaging scans

Keyhan Najafian, Benjamin Rehany, Alexander Nowakowski, Saba Ghazimoghadam, Kevin Pierre, Rita Zakarian, Tariq Al-Saadi, Caroline Reinhold, Abbas Babajani-Feremi, Joshua K. Wong, Marie-Christine Guiot, Marie-Constance Lacasse, Stephanie Lam, Peter M. Siegel, Kevin Petrecca, Matthew Dankner, Reza Forghani

Keyhan Najafian and Benjamin Rehany contributed equally.

Brain metastasis invasion pattern is prognostic but can currently only be read from resected tissue. This study shows it can be predicted from preoperative MRI with 85% accuracy, using radiomic features from the peritumoral edema, in a task where expert neuroradiologists reach only 44 to 59%.

Abstract

Background. Brain metastasis invasion pattern (BMIP) is an emerging biomarker associated with recurrence-free and overall survival in patients, and differential response to therapy in preclinical models. Currently, BMIP can only be determined from the histopathological examination of surgical specimens, precluding its use as a biomarker prior to therapy initiation. The aim of this study was to investigate the potential of machine learning (ML) approaches to develop a noninvasive magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)-based biomarker for BMIP determination. Methods. From an initial cohort of 329 patients, a subset of 132 patients met the inclusion criteria for this retrospective study. We evaluated the ability of an expert neuroradiologist to reliably predict BMIP. Thereafter, the dataset was randomly divided into training/validation (80% of cases) and test subsets (20% of cases). The ground truth for BMIP was the histopathologic evaluation of resected specimens. Following MRI sequence co-registration, advanced feature extraction techniques deriving hand-crafted radiomic features with traditional ML classifiers and convolution-based deep learning (CDL) models were trained and evaluated. Different ML approaches were used individually or using ensembling techniques to determine the model with the best performance for BMIP prediction. Results. Expert evaluation of brain MRI scans could not reliably predict BMIP, with an accuracy of 44% to 59% depending on the semantic feature used. Among the different ML and CDL models evaluated, the best-performing model achieved an accuracy of 85% and an F1 score of 90%. Conclusions. ML approaches can effectively predict BMIP, representing a noninvasive MRI-based approach to guide the management of patients with brain metastases.

Key contributions

  • First study to predict brain metastasis invasion pattern noninvasively from MRI using radiomics and machine learning.
  • Shows that conventional semantic imaging features read by an expert neuroradiologist do not reliably predict invasion pattern.
  • Finds that the peritumoral edema region on T2-weighted images, not the enhancing tumor itself, carries the most predictive signal.
  • Uses patient-wise splits, training-only feature selection, and image-level standardization across two scanners to guard against leakage and scanner bias.

Methods and techniques

  • Radiomics with PyRadiomics (107 hand-crafted features)
  • Support vector classifier, random forest, multi-layer perceptron
  • EfficientNet-B0/B1/B2 convolution-based deep learning
  • Two-level majority-vote ensembling
  • MRI sequence co-registration with ANTs
  • Variance filtering and chi-square feature selection
  • Manual volume-of-interest segmentation in 3D Slicer

Data and materials

  • 329 screened patients, 132 eligible for machine learning analysis
  • Preoperative T2-weighted and post-contrast T1-weighted MRI acquired on 3.0 T Philips and 1.5 T GE Signa scanners
  • Histopathological invasion scoring by two blinded observers as ground truth
  • Source numerical data available on reasonable request; patient images cannot be shared for privacy reasons

Results

  • Best ensemble model: 85% accuracy, 93% precision, 87% recall, 90% F1 score
  • Peritumoral edema features outperformed enhancing tumor features by roughly 18%
  • Expert neuroradiologist accuracy ranged from 44.2% to 59.0% across semantic features
  • Inter-observer agreement for histopathological scoring: 83.7%, Cohen's kappa 0.66

Applications

  • Noninvasive patient stratification before surgery or radiosurgery
  • Surgical planning and stereotactic radiosurgery margin selection
  • Predictive biomarker development for emerging targeted therapies in brain metastases
