Precision Health and Medical Imaging Research

In clinical imaging the constraint is different from agriculture but structurally similar: the ground truth is scarce, expensive, and sometimes only obtainable through an invasive procedure. My precision health research develops machine learning models that recover clinically meaningful information from images that are already acquired as part of standard care, so that a biomarker can be estimated before treatment rather than after surgery.

The work combines hand-crafted radiomic feature extraction with convolution-based deep learning, ensemble aggregation across models and image slices, and careful methodological controls: patient-wise data splits to prevent leakage, feature selection performed only on training data, image-level standardization across scanners, and evaluation on held-out independent test sets.

This research is carried out with the Augmented Intelligence and Precision Health Laboratory at the Research Institute of the McGill University Health Centre and the Radiomics and Augmented Intelligence Laboratory at the University of Florida College of Medicine.

Publications

Machine learning prediction of brain metastasis invasion pattern on brain magnetic resonance imaging scans

Neuro-Oncology Advances 2024 · Keyhan Najafian, Benjamin Rehany, Alexander Nowakowski et al.

Brain metastasis invasion pattern is prognostic but can currently only be read from resected tissue. This study shows it can be predicted from preoperative MRI with 85% accuracy, using radiomic features from the peritumoral edema, in a task where expert neuroradiologists reach only 44 to 59%.
