A Semi-Self-Supervised Approach for Dense-Pattern Video Object Segmentation

Keyhan Najafian, Farhad Maleki, Lingling Jin, Ian Stavness

Extends dense-pattern segmentation from still images to video. Synthetic clips that mimic real camera and object motion are used for pretraining, then pseudo-labeled real video refines the model, which holds up on drone footage across growth stages from boot stage to harvest.

Abstract

Video object segmentation (VOS), predicting pixel-level regions for objects within each frame of a video, is particularly challenging in agricultural scenarios, where videos of crops include hundreds of small, dense, and occluded objects (stems, leaves, flowers, pods) that sway and move unpredictably in the wind. Supervised training is the state-of-the-art for VOS, but it requires large, pixel-accurate, human-annotated videos, which are costly to produce for videos with many densely packed objects in each frame. We train the model first with synthetic data that mimics the camera and object motion of real videos and then with pseudo-labeled videos. We evaluate our DVOS method for wheat head segmentation from a diverse set of videos (handheld, drone-captured, different field locations, and different growth stages, spanning from boot stage to wheat mature and harvest ready). Despite using only a few manually annotated video frames, the proposed approach yielded a high-performing model, achieving a Dice score of 0.79 when tested on a drone-captured external test set. While our method was evaluated on wheat head segmentation, it can be extended to other crops and domains, such as crowd analysis or microscopic image analysis.

Key contributions

  • Defines dense video object segmentation (DVOS) as a distinct problem: hundreds of small, occluded, wind-moved objects per frame.
  • Synthesizes training video that reproduces both camera motion and object motion, not just static appearance.
  • Uses pseudo-labeled real video as the second training stage, requiring only a few annotated frames.
  • Validates across handheld and drone capture, multiple field locations, and growth stages from boot stage to harvest.

Methods and techniques

  • Dense video object segmentation (DVOS)
  • Semi-self-supervised learning
  • Synthetic video generation with motion simulation
  • Pseudo-labeling
  • Spatiotemporal attention
  • Multi-task UNet-style architecture
  • Diffusion-based and temporal modeling of frame sequences
  • External test set evaluation

Data and materials

  • Handheld and drone-captured wheat field video
  • Multiple field locations and growth stages (boot stage through harvest ready)
  • A small number of manually annotated video frames

Results

  • Dice score of 0.79 on a drone-captured external test set

Applications

  • Drone and handheld crop monitoring over time
  • Temporal plant phenotyping and growth-stage tracking
  • Transferable to crowd analysis and microscopy with similar density characteristics

A Semi-Self-Supervised Learning Approach for Wheat Head Detection Using Extremely Small Number of Labeled Samples

ICCVW 2021

Trains a wheat head detector from three contoured images and a few minutes of handheld video. The annotated wheat heads are cut out, augmented, and pasted onto background field footage to simulate a large computationally annotated dataset, and two domain adaptation stages then close the gap to real imagery.

Semi-Self-Supervised Learning for Semantic Segmentation in Images with Dense Patterns

Plant Phenomics 2023

Extends the synthesis-plus-domain-adaptation idea from detection to pixel-level segmentation. Two annotated images and one short video clip are enough to train a customized U-Net that reaches a Dice score of 0.89 internally and generalizes to wheat fields from 18 domains across five countries.

Detection of Fusarium Damaged Kernels in Wheat Using Deep Semi-Supervised Learning on a Novel WheatSeedBelt Dataset

ICCVW 2023

Introduces WheatSeedBelt, a 40,420-image single-kernel dataset covering 268 wheat varieties with damage annotations, and uses it to train semi-supervised classifiers for Fusarium damaged kernel grading. An inter-rater study shows human experts do not outperform the model.
